# Pregnancy and postpartum tracking apps: competitor and gap analysis (global + Turkey), as of 7 October 2026

Method note for the report writer: store data (ratings, rating counts, chart ranks, in-app purchase prices, Apple privacy labels, Google Play Data safety summaries) was pulled live on **2026-10-07** from the Apple App Store web pages, the iTunes Search/Lookup API and Google Play listing pages (Turkey and US storefronts). User-complaint figures come from my own keyword count over the most recent App Store reviews (Apple's public RSS review feed, up to 500 reviews per app, ~8,000 in total). These counts are rough keyword matches, not a validated content analysis. Google Play install counts are **global**, not Turkey-only. Apple privacy labels and Google Data safety forms are filled in by the developers themselves, and Apple says it does not verify them. Anything older than 2025 is dated inline.

## 1. What the top pregnancy apps give free, what is paid, how heavy ads are, and what data they share

### Takeaway
There are three business models. (1) **Ad-funded "free everything"**: What to Expect, BabyCenter and The Bump have no in-app purchases, and their Apple labels say they track users with Sensitive Info and Contact Info. (2) **Freemium subscriptions** that charge for tools like the kick counter, contraction timer, weight log and detailed weekly content: Pregnancy+, Sprout, Flo, Clue and Glow, at roughly $35–60 a year. (3) **Employer-paid**: Ovia. In Turkey the leaders (Happy Mom, Elika, HokkabazSoft's "Gün Gün Gebelik Takibi") run ads and subscriptions together, and ad fatigue is now their main complaint. The only no-ads, no-data app is the Ministry's "Annelik Yolculuğu", which is rated poorly. Sprout is the only major global pregnancy app with a "no tracking, no linked data" Apple label, but it charges for almost everything.

### Inferences
- In the US, free means ad-funded, and the two biggest free apps (WTE, BabyCenter, both Everyday Health) declare cross-app tracking that includes "Sensitive Info". A Turkish privacy-first app that is free at its core and has no tracking would have almost no direct global equivalent. Sprout is the closest on privacy but is subscription-only.
- Putting the kick counter, contraction timer or weight log behind a paywall (Pregnancy+, Sprout) produces visible resentment, because users see them as basic safety tools. Keeping those free and charging for comfort or convenience features is likely the less contested split.
- In the Turkish market, ads are the main source of 1★ reviews for the leading local app (Happy Mom: 42% of recent reviews mention ads). "No intrusive ads" is a credible positioning there. Nilu's own code serves only non-personalized ads with no ATT tracking prompt (`lib/data/datasources/ads_datasource.dart`). Even so, ads shown during pregnancy content are a known irritant, so ad placement in pregnancy mode deserves care.

### Gaps
- I could not open in-app paywalls for BabyCenter, WTE, Flo or Clue, so the feature-level free/paid split is taken from store listings and help pages.
- Hello Belly's and Peanut's Apple privacy labels and prices were not retrieved (page fetch failed).
- There is no source for actual ad frequency (ads per session). The ad-burden evidence is review-based only.

## 2. Must-have vs nice-to-have features

### Takeaway
Users treat these as table stakes: due date and gestational-week calculation, week-by-week development content, and a size comparison. Every leading app has them. Next come the "tools" users expect to be free: kick counter, contraction timer, weight log and appointment calendar or reminders. Their absence or paywalling is what users complain about. 3D visuals, name finders, bump photos, registries, AI chat and gender-prediction games are differentiators or nice-to-haves. **Blood-pressure tracking and danger-sign guidance are almost absent from competitors' store descriptions**, and so is lochia tracking.

### Inferences
- Must-have (free): due date/week calculator with manual correction, weekly development, size comparison, kick counter, contraction timer, weight log, appointment and test reminders. Nice-to-have or premium candidates: 3D, journal/PDF export, bump photos, names, birth plan, partner sharing.
- Nilu's MoH-based danger signs and BP warnings fill a real gap. No Turkish or global competitor's store description mentions danger signs, and only two niche apps mention BP. This should be surfaced in store copy, since competitors compete on descriptions.
- A kick counter tied to the guideline's "reduced fetal movement = danger sign" message is clinically meaningful. That framing is something entertainment-style apps (baby "messages", gender games) don't offer.
- Manual override of the gestational week (from an ultrasound date) is a frequent source of 1★ reviews when missing. It is worth keeping prominent.

### Gaps
- I found no recent large-sample survey that ranks pregnancy-app features by importance. Must-have status is inferred from feature ubiquity and complaint patterns.
- A Turkish study reportedly found **46.9%** of pregnant women used a pregnancy-related m-health app (İzmir, Sep 2019–Jan 2020). The same study reportedly found the top information needs were baby growth (82.9%), labour signs (66.2%) and nutrition (65.7%). I saw these figures only in a search-engine summary and could not retrieve the primary paper to verify them.

## 3. How apps handle pregnancy loss and the return to cycle tracking, and what users say

### Takeaway
Big apps now have a "report a loss" or "I'm no longer pregnant" switch. What to Expect, BabyCenter, Clue, Flo, Glow and The Bump have one, and WTE adds miscarriage support content and a remembrance date. But users keep reporting the same failure: **communications that don't stop**. After a reported loss they still get size-update pushes, "countdown to baby" emails, baby-care packages, formula samples and late-period nags. Flows also miss loss types. Sprout offers miscarriage only (no stillbirth), Flo has no ectopic option, and Ovia's loss side is "almost nonexistent". Dedicated recovery modes exist only at Natural Cycles (since 2022) and Carea (UK, April 2026). **None of the Turkish apps examined mentions loss support in its store description, and none of the sampled Turkish reviews discusses it.**

### Cited Findings
- **What to Expect.** Has a "Report a Loss" option that shows miscarriage support options in-app (Sep 2026 review). Another user praises that WTE remembered her earlier miscarriage and avoided triggering reminders in the next pregnancy (Aug 2026). Failures:
  - a size-update notification arrived after "Report a Loss" (12 Sep 2026);
  - a "baby care package" reminder after a miscarriage (20 Aug 2026);
  - formula samples by post (May 2026);
  - only **one remembrance date** can be stored, and adding a second loss deleted the first (3 Oct 2026, "a little triggering").

  — [WTE reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=289560144/sortby=mostrecent/json)
- **BabyCenter** lists "Loss of pregnancy" support groups and resources as a feature — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id386022579). A stillbirth parent wrote that the app "gives me relief" (Aug 2026) — [reviews](https://itunes.apple.com/us/rss/customerreviews/page=1/id=386022579/sortby=mostrecent/json)
- **Clue.** More menu → "I'm no longer pregnant" → optional reason → support resources → "change your mode once you feel ready". ### Inferences
- The bar for "gentle loss handling" is not the switch itself. It is **a hard stop on every downstream channel**: push notifications, email, partner and marketing feeds, size and countdown widgets, "late period" nags. It also means support for every loss type (early miscarriage, ectopic, molar, termination for medical reasons, stillbirth), keeping or deleting data as the user chooses, more than one remembrance date, and starting a new pregnancy without a logged period. These are exactly the points users name as failures in 2025–26.
- A privacy-first, local-first app has a structural advantage here. With no email list, no partner marketing and no data broker, it avoids the "formula samples after miscarriage" class of harm entirely. That is worth saying explicitly in positioning.
- A recovery mode in the Natural Cycles style fits a cycle-plus-pregnancy app well. It would suppress fertility predictions and period-late alerts, track bleeding or lochia, mood and the first period, and hide the pregnancy "long cycle" from statistics (as Clue advises).
- Nilu's codebase already notes that lochia, milk and postpartum checks apply after stillbirth too (`lib/domain/models/life_stage.dart`). That is beyond what any reviewed competitor documents.
- In Turkey, loss handling is a whitespace. No local app markets it, so a gentle, Turkish-language loss flow would be unique among the apps examined.

### Gaps
- I could not test the BabyCenter, WTE or Flo loss flows hands-on. Their exact options, and whether emails stop, are reconstructed from user reviews.
- I found no Turkish-language qualitative research on pregnancy apps and miscarriage.

## 4. Turkish landscape, MoH antenatal guidance, Annelik Yolculuğu and e-Nabız

### Takeaway
Turkish pregnancy-app demand is served by a few ad-heavy local apps plus localized global apps:
- **Happy Mom:** No. 5 Medical in the TR App Store, 1M+ installs on Play.
- **Elika:** 500K+ installs.
- **HokkabazSoft's "Gün Gün Gebelik Takibi":** 1M+ installs, and its Play Data safety form says data is shared, not encrypted and can't be deleted.
- **Pregnancy+:** No. 6 Medical in TR.

The Ministry of Health has run its own free, no-ads, no-data app **"Annelik Yolculuğu" since May 2025**. Midwives push it through the "Her Gebeye Ebe" programme, but it is poorly rated on iOS (3.41) and criticised for bugs, judgmental caesarean content and no twin support. **e-Nabız has no pregnancy module** in its 2025 user guide. It is a records system (tests, visits, vaccines) with manual BP and weight entry. The official antenatal standard is the 2018 "Doğum Öncesi Bakım Yönetim Rehberi": at least 4 visits (first 14 weeks, 18–24, 24–28, 36–38), 13 named danger signs, and BP thresholds of 140/90 (refer) and 160/110 (urgent).

### Inferences
- **Positioning gap in Turkey.** The two most-installed local apps are ad-funded. Happy Mom has a 2.28 average in recent reviews driven by ads, and Gün Gün has a self-declared "not encrypted / can't delete" data posture. The state app is privacy-clean but buggy and seen as preachy about caesareans. A privacy-first, ad-light Turkish app with MoH-correct schedules and neutral, non-judgmental birth content sits between these.
- **Complement, don't compete, with the state stack.** e-Nabız holds records but offers no pregnancy guidance. Annelik Yolculuğu is general information that explicitly disclaims monitoring. Midwives in "Her Gebeye Ebe" already promote apps, so an app that mirrors the MoH 4-visit, 6-postpartum-visit schedule and uses the same danger-sign vocabulary is easy for clinicians to endorse. A possible feature (needs verification) is a BP/weight export the user can show the midwife, or enter into e-Nabız's sensor-data screen.
- **Paid tier price anchors in TRY.** Local apps cluster at about ₺99.99/month and ₺199.99–999.99/year. Global apps charge ₺699.99–2,499.99/year in Turkey. A "pregnancy pass" priced per trimester or per pregnancy (Clovy ₺999.99 trimester plan, Sprout "Unlimited pregnancies" ₺249.99) is an existing pattern.
- **Concrete unmet needs from Turkish reviews:** twins/multiples, several children or previous pregnancies (Momy already supports previous pregnancy profiles), reliable week calculation with manual correction, no data loss on update, full Turkish content, and no forced ads.
