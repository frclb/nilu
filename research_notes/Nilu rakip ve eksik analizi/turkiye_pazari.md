*Conventions: "App Store TR" data comes from apps.apple.com/tr product and chart pages fetched on 7 Oct 2026. App Store pages show update dates without a year for the current year, so those are assumed to be 2026. Older sources are dated inline. Notes are in English, with Turkish terms kept where useful.*

## Q1. Which period trackers lead in Turkey (App Store / Google Play), with ratings, review counts, localization quality and TRY prices?

### Takeaway
On 7 Oct 2026 the Turkish App Store's Health & Fitness top-free chart was led by a foreign generic tracker ("Period & Cycle Tracker", #4, 4.9★ from ~81K ratings). Next came the Turkish pad brand's app beije (#11), Flo (#15) and the Ministry of Health's pregnancy app Annelik Yolculuğu (#19). Pepapp, the long-time local leader, still has by far the largest Turkish rating base (~242K) but ranks only #33, and its App Store page shows no update since Sep 2024. On Google Play TR, Simple Design's "Adet Takibi - Regl Takvimi" tops the "adet takvimi" search. Local apps tend to be stale or full of ads, and global apps have incomplete Turkish content. A maintained, Turkish-native, privacy-first app with a generous free tier has room to lead.

### Inferences
- **No incumbent meets all the basic needs at once.** The needs are:
  1. fully native Turkish copy and content;
  2. an actively maintained app;
  3. core tracking and predictions free, without ad overload;
  4. credible privacy;
  5. Diyanet-aligned religious features;
  6. a pregnancy and postpartum mode tied to the Ministry of Health (MoH) schedule.
  - Each incumbent covers only part of this. Pepapp has Turkish copy but ads and an apparently stale iOS build. Flo is polished but has English content pockets and tracking. beije is Turkish and has no paywall, but it is a pad-sales funnel with no pregnancy mode. Period & Cycle Tracker is feature-rich but generic and heavy on paywalls and ad removal.
- **What beije's rank suggests.** beije reached #11 with only ~1.3K ratings, so ranking is driven by download velocity (brand marketing) rather than accumulated reviews. A new entrant can rank quickly with good acquisition.
- **Pepapp's opportunity.** Pepapp's 242K ratings show strong, long-standing demand for a Turkish-native tracker. If its iOS app really is unmaintained since Sep 2024, its users are a natural migration pool.
- **Localization bar.** Turkish users notice and complain about English fragments (Flo). A "100% Türkçe" claim (Pepapp's historical selling point) still differentiates. Metadata that lists only "English" for Turkish apps (Annelik Yolculuğu, Elika) reflects sloppy App Store localization.
- **Basic needs a leader must nail** (synthesis across Q1–Q6):
  - accurate predictions that handle irregular cycles, and explain changes in plain Turkish;
  - instant logging;
  - a lock (PIN or biometric), a discreet icon and neutral notifications;
  - core features free;
  - no ads, or no tracking-based ads;
  - optional Diyanet-aligned worship mode: namaz/oruç status, kaza orucu counter, hayız/istihaze/nifas rules;
  - a pregnancy mode that mirrors MoH visits and tests;
  - postpartum (lohusalık) support;
  - TTC and fertile-window support without contraceptive claims;
  - KVKK-compliant explicit consent with on-device or Turkey/EU data storage.

## Q2. Price sensitivity, willingness to pay, payment habits and ad tolerance in Turkey (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
Period-app subscriptions on App Store TR are priced at roughly ₺40–150 per month and ₺100–640 per year, about $0.8–3 per month and $2–13 per year at ~₺49/USD. That is at or below mainstream entertainment subscriptions (Spotify ₺115, YouTube Premium ₺119.99, Netflix Basic ₺259.99 in 2026). Annual inflation is ~30% and the lira keeps weakening, so TRY prices need frequent revision. Turkey's digital services tax (DST) cut from 7.5% to 5% in 2026 (2.5% in 2027) slightly improves developer proceeds. Users openly resent paywalls on basic features and ad overload. Android has about 75% of the market, and Google Play can bill through mobile operators with low limits.

- **USD equivalents** (at ₺49.03):
  - Period & Cycle Tracker monthly ₺39.99 ≈ $0.82
  - Flo's lowest Premium ₺59.99 ≈ $1.22
  - Pepapp monthly ₺149.99 ≈ $3.06
  - Flo's top tier ₺639.99 ≈ $13.05
  - Spotify Individual ₺115 ≈ $2.35
  - Netflix Basic ₺259.99 ≈ $5.30
- **Price ceiling.** Spotify and YouTube Premium (~₺115–120 per month) are the everyday reference points. A period app charging more than that monthly (Pepapp ₺149.99) is likely to feel expensive. Annual plans at ₺250–600 and lifetime or one-time unlocks (₺149.99–₺1,499.99 seen in the category) fit Turkish subscription fatigue.
- **Repricing.** With ~30% annual inflation and a weakening lira, TRY prices lose ~2–2.5% of real value per month. Expect to reprice TRY tiers at least twice a year. Apple's tax-driven adjustments do not automatically raise existing auto-renewable subscription prices.
- **Ads.** Ads in an intimate health category hurt trust and require tracking SDKs, which then appear in privacy labels. Competitors' labels (Flo, Pepapp, Period & Cycle Tracker) show tracking. A "no ads, no tracking" free tier is a cheap differentiator.
- **Payment friction.** Android dominance plus carrier billing (with low limits) means an Android-first launch and low-priced tiers help conversion. Distrust of surprise charges argues for clear trial-end reminders.

## Q3. Cultural, religious and privacy factors (shared phones, worship rules, partners, taboos)

### Takeaway
Menstruation remains partly taboo in Turkey (historically about half of women felt they had to keep it secret), and many women's phones sit inside family or partner control dynamics. This makes a lock, a discreet icon and neutral notifications basic needs, not extras. Religious practice creates a concrete, underserved use case: Diyanet's rulings give clear, computable rules. Hanafi rules are the default, with Şafii and medical exceptions acknowledged:
- **Hayız (menstruation):** 3–10 days, with at least 15 days of tuhr (purity) between periods.
- **Nifas (postpartum bleeding):** at most 40 days (Hanafi) or 60 days (Şafii).
- **Istihaze (non-menstrual bleeding):** bleeding outside these limits.
- **Kaza:** missed fasts are made up; missed prayers are not.

No mainstream Turkish tracker found implements these rules. Partners are involved through pregnancy programs, and withdrawal (geri çekme) is a common contraceptive method. Both point to opt-in partner features, but never mandatory ones.

### Cited Findings
**Religious rulings (Diyanet İşleri Başkanlığı / Din İşleri Yüksek Kurulu)**
- **Hanafi limits:** hayız lasts at least 3 and at most 10 days; the minimum tuhr between periods is 15 days. Bleeding under 3 or over 10 days is istihaze. — [Diyanet DİYK fetva (page dated 13 May 2026)](https://kurul.diyanet.gov.tr/tr/fetva/adet-kanamasi-10-gunden-fazla-suren-bir-kadin-ibadetlerini-yerine-getirmede-nasil-hareket-etmelidir-/0193c42d-4ba9-7e84-ebf0-5084841c2b3e)
- **Bleeding past 10 days with a regular habit (mutad):** if bleeding stays within 10 days, all of it is hayız. If it exceeds 10 days, only the habitual days count as hayız and the rest is istihaze. Example: with a 6-day habit, days 7+ are istihaze. Prayers and fasts missed after the habit ended must be made up (kaza). — [Diyanet DİYK](https://kurul.diyanet.gov.tr/tr/fetva/adet-kanamasi-10-gunden-fazla-suren-bir-kadin-ibadetlerini-yerine-getirmede-nasil-hareket-etmelidir-/0193c42d-4ba9-7e84-ebf0-5084841c2b3e)
- **2017 fetva (dated 12 Jul 2017):** women whose bleeding is regularly under 3 or over 10 days may count bleeding up to 15 days as hayız, if a medical examination supports it. This effectively allows the Şafii view (hayız 1–15 days), and the fetva cites gynecologists' observations. — [Diyanet DİYK](https://kurul.diyanet.gov.tr/tr/fetva/duzenli-olarak-uc-gunden-az-ya-da-on-gunden-fazla-kanamasi/0193c42d-4bc3-7336-8bf6-117b4978b02d)
- **Nifas / lohusalık:**
  - No minimum; maximum 40 days (Hanafi) or 60 days (Şafii). Bleeding beyond that is istihaze.
  - If bleeding stops before 40 days, she performs gusül (ritual bath) and resumes worship.
  - Suspended during nifas: namaz, oruç, tavaf, intercourse.
  - Missed prayers are not made up; missed fasts must be.
  - — [Diyanet fetva](https://fetva.diyanet.gov.tr/tr/fetva/lohusalik-suresi-ne-kadardir-bu-surede-yapilamayan-ibadetlerin-durumu-nedir/0193c42d-4c43-7518-7c50-022c48f28943)
- **Other DİYK rulings ("Kadınlara Özgü Haller" category):**
  - Spotting before or after the habitual days in women using a spiral (IUD) counts as hayız if it does not exceed 10 days.
  - Bleeding after a molar pregnancy or an empty (anembryonic) pregnancy is istihaze.
  - Bleeding after menopause is istihaze.
  - Vaginal discharge is clean, so no protective pad is required for prayer validity.
  - The category also covers whether a gynecological exam or ultrasound requires gusül, and re-bleeding within 15 days after a period.
  - — [Diyanet DİYK – Kadınlara Özgü Haller](https://kurul.diyanet.gov.tr/tr/fetvalar/kadinlara-ozgu-haller/6d3aea8e-4d00-47e7-b289-aeb000d961fa)

### Inferences
- **Basic privacy needs.** In a context of secrecy norms, shared or inspected phones, and partner-perpetrated digital abuse (15.7% of digital-violence cases), the basics are:
  - an app lock with biometrics;
  - an optional disguised icon and name (e.g., a generic "Takvim" icon);
  - notifications with neutral wording ("Hatırlatma", reminder) and nothing about periods or sex on the lock screen;
  - quick-hide;
  - no account required for core use.
- **Sex logging.** Sexual-activity logging should be optional and hideable. Unmarried women may avoid apps that ask about sex by default, given the taboo and the 80%+ disapproval of intercourse during menstruation (2010 data).
- **Religious mode as a differentiator.** An opt-in "İbadet modu" could use Diyanet's rules:
  - show namaz/oruç status;
  - flag days past 10 as istihaze with an explanation;
  - support the mutad (habit) rule;
  - count missed Ramadan fasts as a kaza-orucu balance;
  - track lohusalık up to 40 days with gusül reminders;
  - offer a medical-exception toggle (Diyanet 2017: up to 15 days with a doctor's assessment);
  - offer a Şafii option. Diyanet fatwas cite both Hanafi and Şafii limits.

  It should be framed as informational, citing Diyanet links, with no fatwa-giving.
- **Partners.** Partner features should be strictly opt-in, share minimal data, and be revocable, because the partner is also a potential source of control. They fit the pregnancy and TTC contexts best, where the MoH already involves fathers. Withdrawal's prevalence means many couples want "fertile day" awareness. That must be framed as non-contraceptive information (see Q5 on medical-device risk).
- **Tone.** Copy should be warm and respectful and avoid explicit imagery. Some users, especially older ones, will be conservative.

### Gaps
- No Turkish quantitative data was found on shared-phone use or on how often partners or family inspect women's phones (beyond the digital-violence rates).
- No recent (2023–2026) nationwide data on menstrual secrecy and taboos was found. The main quantitative study is from 2010.
- No data was found on how many Turkish women follow the Şafii madhhab or use religious apps for menstrual rulings.
- No Turkish-language period tracker with hayız/istihaze/nifas logic was found. The search was not exhaustive (App Store fetches were rate-limited).

## Q4. Health-system context (MoH antenatal and postnatal schedules, e-Nabız, health-information seeking, gynecologist visits)

### Takeaway
The MoH standard (2018 guideline) is at least 4 antenatal visits (≤14, 18–24, 24–28 and 36–38 weeks), 6 postpartum follow-ups (3 in hospital, 3 at home) and 42 days of postpartum monitoring, with defined tests and supplements by week. In 2025 the MoH launched its own app, Annelik Yolculuğu, which has e-Devlet login and e-Nabız vaccine integration and ranks #19 but is rated poorly (3.4★). It was released alongside the "Her Gebeye Bir Ebe" program and the Normal Birth Action Plan. Fertility is at a record low (TFR 1.42 in 2025) and the state is pronatalist ("Aile ve Nüfus On Yılı", 2026–2035). Preventive gynecological care is low: 59% of women have never had a smear test. Women rely heavily on the internet and rarely verify information with professionals.

### Inferences
- **Pregnancy mode.** A pregnancy mode that mirrors the MoH schedule (visit windows, test weeks, iron, vitamin D and folic acid timing, the 42-day lohusalık period) will feel "official" and trustworthy. Turkish women already get e-Nabız/MHRS-style reminders, and an independent app can do this with a better UX and more inclusive content (cesarean-respectful, multiple pregnancies), filling the gaps users report in Annelik Yolculuğu.
- **Annelik Yolculuğu: competitor and benchmark.** It ranks well despite a 3.4★ rating, likely because of official promotion. A private app should complement it rather than imitate it: focus on the cycle and TTC phases before pregnancy, emotional support, and an inclusive tone.
- **Screening reminders.** Low screening rates (59% never had a smear) make reminders a meaningful basic feature: smear/HPV, breast self-exam, annual gynecology check. Pair them with gentle, de-stigmatizing copy and "what to expect at the gynecologist" content to address exam anxiety.
- **Medically reviewed content.** Women search online but rarely verify with professionals. Turkish content reviewed by gynecologists or midwives, with sources cited, is a trust lever.
- **Pronatalist policy.** It may raise interest in TTC and pregnancy features, while making data about pregnancy loss or termination extremely sensitive given restricted abortion access. Do not collect it by default, store it encrypted, and allow deletion.

## Q5. Regulation (KVKK health data, explicit consent, 2024 cross-border rules, app-store requirements)

### Takeaway
Under KVKK (Law 6698), health and sexual-life data are special-category data. After the 2024 reform (Law 7499, in force 1 Jun 2024), a consumer period app will in practice need specific, informed, freely given explicit consent (açık rıza), unbundled from the terms of service, plus the Board's "adequate measures". Cross-border transfers (for example to US or EU cloud) now require one of:
- an adequacy decision (none reported as issued);
- appropriate safeguards (unmodifiable KVKK standard contracts notified within 5 business days, BCRs or undertakings);
- narrow incidental-transfer grounds.

Consent no longer legitimizes regular transfers. Fines in 2026 reach ~₺17.1M per violation type. No Turkey-specific App Store rule for health apps was found. Global store rules apply: Google Play's Health apps declaration and Apple's health-data rules.

### Cited Findings
- **Law 7499:** published in the Official Gazette on 12 Mar 2024 (No. 32487). The KVKK amendments entered into force on 1 Jun 2024. Explicit consents obtained for international transfers were valid until 1 Sep 2024. — [Erdem & Erdem](https://www.erdem-erdem.av.tr/bilgi-bankasi/kisisel-verilerin-korunmasi-kanununda-neler-degisti); [Hukuki Haber](https://www.hukukihaber.net/kvk-kanununda-ozel-nitelikli-kisisel-verilerin-islenmesi-ve-yurt-disina-veri-aktarimi-kapsaminda-yapilan-degisikliklere-iliskin-degerlendirmeler)
- **Amended Article 6** keeps the definition of special-category data (including health, sexual life and biometric/genetic data) but sets 8 processing grounds:
  1. explicit consent;
  2. explicitly provided by law;
  3. vital interest when consent is impossible;
  4. data made public by the person;
  5. establishing, exercising or defending a right;
  6. processing by persons under a confidentiality obligation for health purposes;
  7. employment, occupational health and social-security duties;
  8. foundations, associations and unions, for their members.
  - Explicit consent must be specific (belirli konuya ilişkin), informed (bilgilendirilmeye dayanan) and freely given (özgür irade). Relying on consent when another legal ground exists is unlawful. Consent must not be a precondition of service.
  - Controllers must apply the Board's "yeterli önlemler" (adequate measures; Board Decision 2018/10 of 31 Jan 2018).
  - The KVKK guide "Özel Nitelikli Kişisel Verilerin İşlenmesine İlişkin Rehber" (2025) explains the new conditions.
  - — [Alomaliye summary of KVKK guide](https://www.alomaliye.com/2025/03/03/ozel-nitelikli-kisisel-verilerin-islenmesine-iliskin-rehber/)
- Without explicit consent, health and sexual-life data may be processed only by persons under a confidentiality obligation, or by authorized public bodies, for public health, preventive medicine, diagnosis, treatment, care, and health-service planning and management. — [Hukuki Haber](https://www.hukukihaber.net/kvk-kanununda-ozel-nitelikli-kisisel-verilerin-islenmesi-ve-yurt-disina-veri-aktarimi-kapsaminda-yapilan-degisikliklere-iliskin-degerlendirmeler)
- **Cross-border transfers (Article 9 and its regulation):**
  - The regulation "Kişisel Verilerin Yurt Dışına Aktarılmasına İlişkin Usul ve Esaslar Hakkında Yönetmelik" was published in the Official Gazette on 10 Jul 2024 (No. 32598).
  - Standard contracts must be notified to KVKK within 5 business days of signature.
  - An online Standard Contract Notification Module was introduced by Board Decision 2024/1793 (17 Oct 2024).
  - — [Alomaliye](https://www.alomaliye.com/2024/10/30/kvkk-standart-sozlesme-bildirim-modulu/); [Webrazzi, 10 Jul 2024](https://webrazzi.com/2024/07/10/kvkk-dan-yeni-yonetmelik-kisisel-veriler-yurt-disina-aktarilabilecek/)
- **The three transfer routes:**
  1. Adequacy decision (country, sector or international organization; re-evaluated every 4 years).
  2. Appropriate safeguards: Board standard contracts (mandatory text that cannot be modified), BCRs, or written undertakings. Templates were published under Board Decision 2024/959 (4 Jun 2024).
  3. Incidental (non-regular, one-off) transfers, e.g., with explicit consent or when necessary for a contract.
  - — [Erdem & Erdem, 10 Jul 2024](https://www.erdem-erdem.av.tr/bilgi-bankasi/kisisel-verilerin-yurt-disina-aktarilmasinda-yasanan-son-gelismeler)
- No country had received an adequacy decision as of the cited source, so appropriate safeguards are the practical route. Consent-based transfer is limited to the incidental route from 1 Sep 2024. — [Mıhcı Law](https://mihci.av.tr/kisisel-verilerin-yurt-disina-aktarilmasi/); [EY Türkiye](https://www.ey.com/tr_tr/insights/law/kvkk-degisikliginin-yurt-disi-veri-aktarimina-etkisi)
- **2026 fine ranges** (2026 revaluation rate 25.49%): — [Cottgroup](https://cottgroup.com/tr/mevzuat/item/yeniden-degerleme-oranina-gore-2026-yili-kvkk-idari-para-cezalari)

  | Violation | 2026 fine range |
  |-----------|-----------------|
  | Duty to inform (aydınlatma) | ₺85,437 – ₺1,709,200 |
  | Data security | ₺256,357 – ₺17,092,242 |
  | Non-compliance with Board decisions | ₺427,263 – ₺17,092,242 |
  | VERBİS registration | ₺341,809 – ₺17,092,242 |

- **VERBİS:** foreign data controllers can be obliged to register in VERBİS (the data controllers' registry) through a data controller representative. Registration must happen before processing begins. — [Cottgroup](https://cottgroup.com/en/legislation/item/yurt-disinda-yerlesik-veri-sorumlularinin-verbis-e-kayit-yukumlulugu-ve-sartlari)
- **App stores (global rules, not Turkey-specific):**
  - Google Play: since 2024, every app (and in particular those in Health & Fitness or Medical) must complete the Health apps declaration in Play Console, and menstrual-cycle tracking is a listed health feature. - **Medical-device precedent:** Natural Cycles is a CE-marked medical device and the only FDA-cleared contraceptive app. Contraceptive claims put an app into medical-device territory. — [Pharmaceutical Journal](https://pharmaceutical-journal.com/article/letters/natural-cycles-does-not-merely-claim-to-be-a-contraceptive-it-is-a-ce-marked-medical-device); [Webrazzi, 30 May 2024](https://webrazzi.com/2024/05/30/natural-cycles/)

### Inferences
- **Compliance architecture.** A compliant MVP needs:
  - a layered aydınlatma metni (privacy notice) in Turkish;
  - separate, granular explicit-consent toggles for health and cycle data, sexual-activity data, and any optional sharing or analytics, not bundled with the terms of service and revocable in-app;
  - the 2018/10 "adequate measures" (encryption, access controls, logging, staff confidentiality);
  - a VERBİS assessment.
- **Data location.** Storing data on-device or in Turkey avoids most Article 9 friction. Using foreign cloud or analytics (US/EU) means signing KVKK standard contracts with each processor and notifying KVKK within 5 business days. Firebase, Mixpanel and ad SDKs each count as a transfer. Fewer SDKs means less paperwork and an easier privacy label.
- **Privacy as marketing.** Competitors' labels show tracking, and Turkish users ask whether their data is sold. A "no third-party tracking, data stays on your phone or in Turkey" message is both a compliance simplification and a selling point.
- **Avoid contraceptive claims.** Fertile-window or "safe day" features should be presented as informational, not contraceptive, or they risk medical-device regulation. Turkey's medical-device framework is understood to follow the EU MDR, but this needs legal confirmation.
- **Religious data.** Worship status could be treated as religious-belief data, which is also special category under KVKK. Opt-in consent and local storage for the religious mode are prudent.

### Gaps
- KVKK's adequacy-decision status as of Oct 2026 was not re-verified.
- The exact current VERBİS thresholds and exemptions for foreign app developers with no Turkish entity were not confirmed.
- TİTCK rules on software as a medical device (SaMD), and whether any fertility-awareness app is registered in Turkey, were not found.
- No KVKK Board decisions specifically about menstrual or femtech apps were found.
- The MoH regulation on personal health data ("Kişisel Sağlık Verileri Hakkında Yönetmelik") and the telehealth regulation (relevant if adding doctor chat) were not reviewed.
- No Turkey-specific App Store or Google Play requirement for health apps was found. This was a negative search result, not a confirmed absence.

## Q6. Islamic / Muslim period-tracking apps worldwide (features and reception)

### Takeaway
Since 2025 a wave of small, English-only "Muslim period tracker" apps has appeared, including Sila, MyHayd, Tuhr, flowdays, Nisa Care, Ayda and Dhibic. They compute hayd vs istihada by madhhab, show prayer and fasting status, count missed (qada) fasts and prayers, track nifas and remind about ghusl. Reception is still niche: rating counts are in the single or double digits. None supports Turkish, and none found is tied to Diyanet. That leaves a clear gap for a Turkish-first tracker with an optional, Diyanet-referenced worship mode.

### Cited Findings
- **Sila: Muslim Period Tracker** (developer Imran Qureshi): released 27 Apr 2025; v1.0.5 (12 Jul 2025); 1.0★ from a single rating; $3.99/month or $24.99/year; English only.
  - Features: hayd vs istihadha by madhhab (Hanafi, Shafi'i, Maliki, Hanbali); daily prayer and fasting status; nifas tracking with purity alerts; ghusl reminders; Ramadan fast tracking, make-up planning and fidyah calculation.
  - Claims it does not sell data, but its label links health data to identity.
  - — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6760947127)
- **MyHayd App** (MyHayd App Services Inc.): released 18 Apr 2025; v4.3.0 (updated early Oct 2026); 4.4★ from 19 ratings (US store); free; English only.
  - Hanafi only; hayd, tuhr and istihadah calculations; built under muftis' guidance, with 1,000+ real-life scenarios hand-verified; WhatsApp access to female scholars.
  - Reviews praise its thoroughness and scholar support.
  - — [App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/myhayd-app/id6739942459)
- **Tuhr:** a "private, on-device" companion combining cycle tracking with guidance on prayer, fasting and purity, with data kept entirely on the phone (search description). — [mwm.ai listing](https://mwm.ai/apps/tuhr/6782219409)
- **flowdays – Muslim Cycle Tracker:** distinguishes period from istihadah and tells users when to pray, fast and make ghusl, based on fiqh (search description). — [App Store IT](https://apps.apple.com/it/app/flowdays/id6756862056)
- **Nisa Care:** "Islamic wellness app" with automatic prayer exemptions during hayd and a qada (make-up prayer) counter (search description). — [mwm.ai listing](https://mwm.ai/apps/nisa-care/6767461961)

### Inferences
- **Validated feature set.** These apps establish a common set of features:
  - madhhab selector;
  - automatic hayız/istihaze classification from bleeding-day logs, including the 10- and 15-day rules and the habit (mutad) rule;
  - daily "namaz/oruç" status;
  - a Ramadan kaza-orucu counter;
  - nifas mode;
  - gusül reminders;
  - optional scholar Q&A.

  For Turkey these should be localized to Diyanet's rulings, with Hanafi as default and Şafii plus the medical-exception option, and should link to Diyanet fetvas instead of issuing new ones.
- **Execution gap.** Low ratings and English-only builds show the niche is not yet won globally. A Turkish app could lead locally by embedding this as an optional mode inside a high-quality mainstream tracker, rather than as a separate "religious app". That avoids alienating secular users.
- **Privacy positioning.** Islamic apps lead with privacy messaging (Tuhr: on-device; Sila: "doesn't sell data"). This matches Turkish users' concerns (Q3, Q5).

### Gaps
- No independent reviews, press coverage or download data on how these Islamic apps are received were found.
- Only Sila and MyHayd were verified through store pages; the others come from search descriptions.
- No Turkish-language Islamic period tracker was found. App Store TR searches were rate-limited (HTTP 429), so the absence is not definitive.
- No academic evaluation of fiqh accuracy in these apps was reviewed. One Malaysian journal article surfaced ([MyJICT](https://myjict.uis.edu.my/index.php/journal/article/download/227/135/762)) but was not read.
